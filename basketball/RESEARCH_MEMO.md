# Basketball Shooting Mechanics and Movement Consistency

## Research questions
How much of the variation in selected shooting-motion measures is between athletes rather than between shots by the same athlete? Do pose-only features help retrospectively classify made and missed free throws beyond simple rate baselines?

## Source and repeated-measures design
MLSE Sport Performance Lab's SPL Open Data provides 583 real free throws from five non-professional participants. The 2025-12-18 session has 458 trials at 60 Hz across all five participants. The 2024-08-28 session has 125 trials at 30 Hz from P0001 only. Pose models differ between sessions. The main analysis therefore uses the five-person 2025 session; the older session is reported separately and is not pooled into the generalization experiments. The same participant ID across sessions denotes the same person, not an independent sixth participant.

Twenty 2025 trials have marker gaps that remain after short-gap repair and are excluded. This leaves **438 main-analysis trials**; all 125 older trials are retained, for 563 eligible trials in total. Exclusions are logged by trial. Requiring complete selected markers across a clip is conservative and can introduce selection effects; the missingness rule was not chosen by shot outcome.

## Data management and phase alignment
The project preserves full-precision source values for 13 selected body markers, ball coordinates, timestamps, and trial metadata in compact NPZ bundles. It omits unused finger, face and foot markers; it does not fabricate or downsample input movements. Every original JSON file has a provenance hash. Source coordinates are in feet and are converted to metres; timestamps are milliseconds and are converted to seconds. Original outcome landing coordinates are inches and are not used as predictors.

Internal gaps of at most three frames are interpolated; this corresponds to different durations at 30 and 60 Hz. Larger gaps and missing endpoints are not filled. Savitzky–Golay smoothing uses a nominal 0.15-second window and a quadratic polynomial. Sensitivity checks use nominal 0.10 and 0.25-second windows.

The alignment anchor is the peak positive vertical velocity of the smoothed bilateral mean wrist position, restricted to elevated-arm frames with a complete -0.6 to +0.4 second window. It is a **movement-phase proxy, not an annotated or validated ball-release instant**. Both arms are averaged; no shooting-handedness label is inferred. Smoothed trajectories are interpolated to 101 equal time points. Angle features are 3D included joint angles, not clinical measures or biomechanical ground truth. A different pose estimator, anchor, or smoothing method could change the results.

## Multilevel consistency analysis
For wrist speed, minimum knee angle, elbow angle, and relative wrist height, a random-intercept model includes participant as a grouping variable and within-participant trial progress as a fixed covariate. Models use REML. Conditional ICC is participant-intercept variance divided by the sum of participant and residual variances. The wrist-speed ICC is 0.981. This describes variance partitioning among the five observed people after a linear progress adjustment. It is not an estimate of measurement-system accuracy, a causal athlete effect, or a test-retest reliability result across sessions. Only five groups are available, so variance components are fragile; no population-level confidence interval or significance claim is made. Residual shot-to-shot serial dependence is not fully modeled.

## Functional description
PCA is applied to the vectorized aligned elbow, knee, and relative-wrist-height curves, with channel scaling, using all eligible 2025 trials. PC1 accounts for 69.6% and PC2 for 16.3% of variation in this representation. This is descriptive PCA of sampled trajectories, not a trained neural representation or an outcome predictor. Participant separation can reflect anatomy, technique, pose measurement differences, or the chosen normalization. The mean-trajectory shaded bands show within-participant standard deviations, not confidence intervals.

## Outcome labels and honest evaluation
Made/missed labels are supplied in the source. Seven pose-only features summarize movement speed, angles, relative height, and coordination timing. Ball landing coordinates, entry angle, ball trajectory, trial result, participant ID, and session ID are excluded from the learned predictor matrix. Features include the whole selected motion window and the anchor is determined from the clip, so this is retrospective classification; it is not real-time pre-release prediction.

A chronological split trains on the first 70% of eligible shots within each 2025 participant and evaluates the remaining 30% (134 held-out shots). Baselines are the pooled training make rate and each athlete's training make rate. Pose models are standardized regularized logistic regression and a depth-limited random forest with fixed hyperparameters. No test-based tuning is performed. In this split, Brier scores (lower is better) are 0.206 for the athlete-rate baseline, 0.203 for pose logistic regression, and 0.213 for the random forest. The small difference from the strong athlete-rate baseline does not establish practical usefulness.

Leave-one-athlete-out evaluation trains on four people and tests the fifth, repeated across all five (438 pooled out-of-fold shots). The 2024 data are excluded to prevent P0001 leaking into the other-athlete training set. Pooled Brier scores are 0.238 for the training-rate baseline, 0.229 for logistic regression, and 0.224 for random forest. Out-of-fold predictions and participant identifiers are supplied for reanalysis. AUC pooled across folds mixes models and varying athlete base rates; it should not be interpreted as a single calibrated model's universal discrimination. Five held-out people are too few for broad generalization claims.

## Limitations and next questions
These results do not identify optimal technique or show that changing a feature improves accuracy. Motion capture is a reference measurement with its own error. Source ball tracking can be noisy near capture boundaries; it is retained for provenance but excluded from modeling. The older session changes both date and pose system and contains one athlete; session effects cannot be cleanly separated. No inference about injury, professional selection, demographic fairness, or coaching efficacy is supported. Future work should use more athletes and sessions, expert-validated release labels, alternative phase definitions, temporal dependence models, nested validation, and prospective evaluation.
